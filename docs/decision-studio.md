# Decision Studio

Route: `/decisions/`. User approved publication to `kunalkapoor.fyi` on October 7, 2026. Navigation, page metadata and sitemap use Decision Studio as the public page name.

## Simple buy-versus-rent calculator

`/decisions/buy-vs-rent/` now answers three plain-language questions: monthly bills, cash needed upfront, and wealth after investing the difference. The public page uses `SimpleHousingInputs` and `modelSimpleHousing` from `housing-model.ts`; the full engine remains available for existing model checks. The public page has no rate-path editor, break-even searches, property-type switches, inclusion toggles, repeated moves or expense-inflation controls.

The six basic inputs are home price, comparable monthly rent, down payment, constant mortgage rate, full mortgage amortization (1–40 years), and comparison duration (1–100 years). All 40 currency labels remain available, without conversion. Canadian semiannual or monthly nominal mortgage compounding is selected in an optional disclosure.

Recurring insurance, property tax, strata/HOA fees and maintenance are entered as **monthly cash amounts**. Annual quotes must be divided by 12. Rent is monthly too. The monthly table includes the full mortgage payment, with first-year average interest and principal shown separately. Property tax and strata fees can both apply; users exclude amounts already included in another fee. Utilities are assumed equal and excluded. There is no automatic suppression of condo costs or regional preset.

Upfront cash for buying = down payment + closing fees + moving; renting = initial moving costs. Only one move at the start is included. Closing fees cover legal, inspection, transfer-tax and other cash-paid buying fees. Optional selling fees are paid at the comparison end. All optional cash amounts and both growth rates start at zero and reset to zero.

Costs over the selected stay are shown separately from the monthly cash budget. Renting costs = rent + insurance + initial move. Buying costs = actual amortized mortgage interest + insurance + property tax + strata/HOA fees + maintenance + closing/moving/selling fees. Down payment and principal are excluded from this expense total because they build equity. Average monthly costs divide these totals, including one-time fees, by the full comparison months.

Both paths begin with the same cash, the larger upfront requirement. After paying upfront costs, each invests unused cash. They use the same monthly budget: the cheaper path invests its actual monthly saving after existing investments compound. The mortgage payment, including principal, is used when computing these cash savings. Payments stop at payoff, and freed savings are invested thereafter. Investment return is an effective annual after-fee/after-tax assumption converted to its monthly equivalent. Home value grows at the separately entered annual rate. Renting ends with market investments; buying ends with home value minus mortgage and selling fees plus buyer investments. Negative equity is preserved. At zero market return and flat home prices, the final wealth advantage exactly equals the opposite cost difference.

The page explains renter starting capital, later contributions and market gain/loss; it also reconciles buyer home equity and investments. A single optional yearly wealth chart, annual table and CSV provide detail. CSV includes monthly input units, flat-cost rules, cost totals and yearly investment contribution/gain reconciliation. Inflation, renewal changes, repeated moves, refundable deposits, financed mortgage insurance, mortgage tax deductions and home-sale taxes are excluded. Assumptions are never saved or transmitted.

Validation: `node --test tests/housing-model.test.mjs`, targeted ESLint, TypeScript and production export. Browser checks cover monthly units, principal-versus-interest explanations, upfront costs, zero and negative returns, payoff, cash purchase, invalid inputs, currency labels, disclosures, charts/export and phone-width overflow.

## Comparison landing page and car calculator

`/decisions/` now presents linked home and car cards. The existing housing calculator lives at `/decisions/buy-vs-rent/`; `/decisions/buy-vs-lease/` compares keeping one purchased car over a chosen driving duration with repeating complete leases over the same period. Both pages link back to all comparisons. The car result appears before inputs on small screens.

Car costs use tax-inclusive user quotes and monthly loan amortization. Buy cost is upfront cash plus payments and running costs plus remaining debt minus net resale proceeds. Lease cost is upfront cash plus all monthly payments, running costs, prorated excess mileage and return fees. The first lease payment is included in the monthly total and must not be entered again upfront. Running costs and fees start at zero; other defaults are labeled examples. Refundable deposits, early termination, buyout, replacement purchases and business tax deductions are excluded. Repeat leases use identical entered contract assumptions. Currency changes labels only. Run `node --test tests/car-model.test.mjs tests/housing-model.test.mjs` for model verification.

## Car wealth analysis

Car comparisons now match the housing model’s equal-cash and equal-monthly-budget method. Starting funds equal the larger upfront payment. Each path invests its unused starting funds. Existing investments compound at the equivalent monthly rate of the entered after-fee/after-tax annual return, then the cheaper path invests the difference in actual monthly costs at month-end. Mileage and return fees enter each lease-end month’s cash costs and savings; repeat upfront costs enter each new lease’s first month. Negative equity and wealth remain negative. Buy wealth = net resale proceeds + buyer investments − remaining debt; lease wealth = lessee investments after return. At 0% investment return the wealth difference equals lease total cost minus buy total cost.

Running-cost inflation applies to both annual expense budgets at each anniversary; loan/lease payments, upfront costs and lease-end fee estimates remain fixed. Investment return and inflation default to zero. Driving duration (1–360 months), loan term and lease term (1–120 months) are independent; loan payments stop at payoff. Driving duration must be a whole multiple of the lease term. Car resale remains an explicit end-of-term estimate, not a lease residual or forecast. Tax-inclusive price/payment/fee quotes are required. The owned car’s mileage effect must be reflected in resale and expense estimates.

Charts show yearly milestones and a possibly shorter final period: net worth or period cash outflows. Intermediate car value is a straight-line illustration from the all-in purchase price to the entered net resale proceeds; these milestones do not represent early-exit outcomes, and taxes/purchase fees are not claimed to be recoverable. Cash charts include upfront cash and actual lease-end fees, but exclude investments, sale proceeds and final loan settlement. Annual breakdowns and monthly CSV exports use the same model.

Break-even lease payment (0–100,000/month) and net resale (0–100 million) solve final wealth equality with other assumptions held fixed. No crossing is explicitly reported. Thresholds can be applied to the inputs. FCAC’s official car financing options guide informs the contract/cost explanation; the calculator does not claim parity with the government calculator.

## Driving duration and lease mileage

“How long will you drive the car?” sets the independent comparison horizon. Duration shortcuts select one to four complete lease terms, within 30 years. Buying retains the original car for that entire duration. Leasing repeats the quoted payment, non-refundable upfront cash, annual allowance, excess-km price and return-fee assumptions. No new upfront charge is paid after the last return. Running-cost inflation continues across lease changes. The entered resale value must describe the original purchased car at the selected horizon; changing duration preserves it for the user to revise. Incomplete final leases are rejected instead of silently omitting outstanding obligations or inventing early-termination charges.

Expected annual kilometres and the annual allowance appear only under leasing. They affect only lease penalties; buying has no mileage cap or excess-kilometre charge. Users should reflect actual use in purchase resale and running-cost estimates. Tests cover repeat upfront/return events, payoff, equal-budget reconciliation across multiple leases, mileage independence of purchase costs and 30-year horizons.

## Additional comparisons

Two financial simulations also extend the landing page to six tools:
- `/decisions/smith-manoeuvre/`: a fixed 360-month mortgage/HELOC/investment model. Opening balances earn income and growth before month-end re-advances. The annual refund uses prior-year HELOC interest. The selected headline year is independent of the 30-year payoff, interest-saved, refund and peak-HELOC statistics. Reference defaults produce a $277,453 advantage at year 20, mortgage payoff in month 220 versus month 300, $70,717 mortgage interest saved and $143,215 refunds over 30 years. Cumulative positions deduct external cash costs and scheduled payments; they are not household net worth. Full deductibility, constant rates and returns, and no returns on freed payments are explicit simplifications. Lending limits are flagged, not enforced. Capitalized compound interest has a separate tax-treatment caveat and CRA/FCAC references.
- `/decisions/implied-cost-of-equity/`: constant-payout reverse equity valuation with 1–20 explicit years, terminal reinvestment, linked or fixed terminal ROE, optional beta, growth sensitivity and CSV. Example inputs from the supplied brief are labelled unverified, without a current stock-data claim. Default linked ROE solves 8.87%; fixed 13.5% terminal ROE solves 10.92%. Bisection requires a valid rate above perpetual growth and zero, up to 150%; unsupported assumptions and missing brackets are explicit. Terminal growth may track the risk-free input or be entered separately. Sensitivity cells apply both growth assumptions and unlink perpetual growth. No market data, consensus forecasts, residual-income valuation or CAPM estimate is supplied. Tests: `tests/financial-simulations.test.mjs`.

Two independent routes extend the landing page to four comparisons:
- `/decisions/gas-hybrid-electric/`: cash-purchase costs for gas, conventional non-plug-in hybrid and electric cars. Same duration and distance; tax-inclusive prices, confirmed rebates, energy consumption, weighted home/public electricity prices, optional charging setup, maintenance, insurance and net resale. No financing, opportunity-cost investments or inflation. Intermediate car values are explicitly illustrative straight-line estimates.
- `/decisions/debt-vs-invest/`: fixed regular payment plus monthly surplus; apply surplus to principal or investments. Both invest freed budgets after payoff. Nominal debt rate compounds monthly; effective after-tax investment returns compound at the equivalent monthly rate. Reject non-amortizing regular payments.

All expose yearly results, methods, editable currency labels and CSV containing assumptions and model data. Optional numeric costs and investment return start at zero. Example prices and energy rates are hypothetical. Tests in `tests/expansion-models.test.mjs` cover budgets, payoff, compounding, units and charging shares.

## Vehicle insurance, maintenance presets and battery/range inputs

Distance, fuel consumption, and EV range/battery inputs remain in the basic form. Insurance, routine maintenance, and battery replacement reserves are grouped in a collapsed Advanced estimates section. Insurance remains user-quoted, with zero defaults and a visible prompt to enter amounts. Routine scheduled maintenance now defaults from Argonne ANL/ESD-21/4 (2021), Figure 3.28: US$0.101/mile ICE, $0.094 conventional HEV, $0.061 BEV. For 15,000 km, convert distance by 1.609344 km/mile and use an explicitly illustrative selected-currency/USD multiplier of 1.35. No inflation adjustment or live FX claim. Reload recalculates routine amounts at current mileage/multiplier; edits remain until reset/reload.

Hybrid/EV battery reserves are separate to avoid counting replacement in scheduled servicing. Initial replacement budgets are selected planning examples: US$3,500 hybrid (4,725 at the illustrative multiplier) and US$10,500 EV (14,175), the midpoint of Recurrent's December 2024 US$5,000–16,000 range. Fifteen-year reserve funding is an editable planning horizon, not observed life or failure probability. Annual cost = budget/funding years, included as a planning allowance; set budget to zero to exclude. This allowance is not actual cash repair spending. Warranty coverage should reduce out-of-pocket budgeting. Historical estimates are not generic fleet forecasts. Sources are linked in the method disclosure.

EV energy uses usable battery kWh, range per full charge, and wall-to-battery charging efficiency. Wall kWh/km = usable kWh / range / efficiency fraction. Gas and non-plug-in hybrids retain L/100 km. The result labels planning cost because annual battery reserves are included.

## Extra EMI schedule

Debt vs invest defaults to one extra EMI per comparison year, paid in month 12. Users can edit the EMI count and month (relative to projection start), or switch to a monthly surplus. Extra EMI amount tracks the regular payment. Both paths have identical cash budgets and timing; the invest path invests the extra at month-end, and all unused payments are invested after payoff. Annual budgets continue after payoff. Prepayment penalties are excluded. Interest savings and payoff comparisons appear in results, with savings limited to the selected horizon.
